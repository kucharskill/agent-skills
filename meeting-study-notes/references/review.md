# Fresh reviewer task

## Skip-review mode

When asked to verify that no note should be created, inspect every source core and ledger sequentially, without requiring a draft. Independently assess whether any explanation, distinction, example, exercise, assignment, assessment instruction, exam information, or deadline is worth retaining. Do not judge by length or the opening alone. Return either a supported skip decision with coverage and reasons, or concrete overlooked material that warrants a note. Distinguish lack of useful content from unreadable or incomplete input. Keep the report in project `.tmp/`; a confirmed skip produces no final note or image directory and must not remove any pre-existing note. Missing or unhelpful screenshots alone never justify skipping useful text.

## Note-review mode

Assess a note written by another author. You receive SKILL.md, the note standard, source manifest, chunks, extraction ledgers, and draft. Do not assume the ledgers are complete. Do not edit the note files. Save a separate report and return concise findings with proposed corrections.

1. Read every core range sequentially, not just a beginning-and-end sample. Compare source, ledger, and note, section by section when needed. Identify important information, examples, and exceptions lost during extraction or synthesis. Review the reasons for omitting important terms or distinctions, especially when they were treated briefly: check context and available slides, and require only the source-supported scope, not textbook expansion. Distinguish removal of an uncertain definition or claim from removal of a supported name: the name may warrant a short mention without a definition. Check that an unresolved important name was not masked by a generic paraphrase in a complete-looking result; apply the existing uncertainty and final-delivery rules.
2. Check reasoning, conditions, and attribution. Look for student answers turned into established claims, invented conclusions, overgeneralization, and unsupported certainty. Check internal uncertainty records against the rendered text: hidden processing doubts must not turn into unqualified claims or misleading omissions. Agreement between two agents is not evidence.
3. Assess standalone clarity and educational usefulness: developed explanations, meaningful exercises, and revision support. Do not reward length or section count alone. Reject definitions, expansions, examples, or substantive corrections added from general knowledge or the internet rather than speech or supplied visual material. External verification supports term recognition or assessment of a doubt, not additional note content. Do not demand outside material merely to turn the note into a textbook.
4. Check that visible text contains no timestamps, raw speaker labels, AI narrative, or verification report. All transcription doubts, confidence labels, verification requests, and agent/reviewer remarks belong exclusively in valid HTML comments or separate review files, never rendered prose, captions, or alt text. Source-supported conditions remain ordinary content. Check that uncertain interpretations were resolved, narrowed to supported wording, or omitted with an internal record; a materially misleading omission blocks final delivery. Check that headings, explanations, and recall questions follow the consuming project's selected language, rather than defaulting to the skill's English.

5. If images are included, inspect every selected image and its relevant original screenshot and transcript excerpt. Check educational or evidentiary value, accurate captions, preserved substantive titles, legends and task data after cropping, readability, placement, portable relative links, and the 10-image maximum. Distinguish substantive headings from clearly separate empty editor fields or template instructions: the latter may be removed by cropping if all educational content, conditions, and necessary context survive. Do not demand retouching of editor marks within content or removal of every aesthetic trace of the source; compare the original and saved crop rather than treating any original placeholder as necessary content. Check project output and filename conventions and shared versus dedicated image folders. Check consistency between the text and the concepts shown in included illustrations; an important label must not disappear from the text solely because the spoken treatment was brief, but do not demand explanations of every label. Time proximity alone does not establish what a speaker referred to. Do not require images when none are useful or open video to verify them. If optional visual inspection cannot be completed, omit unverified illustrations rather than implying they were reviewed.

6. Check that all supported assignments, assessment requirements, tests, exams, and deadlines appear in a distinct section below the title's meeting-details line, before the substantive body. Verify exact conditions and dates against the source, including later corrections or cancellations. Confirm that proposals and unresolved questions are not presented as settled instructions, missing details were not invented, and an empty section was not added without relevant source content. Flag any important assessment threshold, scope, deadline, or eligibility condition that remains uncertain after checking context and supplied materials. Approval of not guessing is not resolution: require the author to name the gap and completeness limit in the external handoff and retain draft status under the existing final-delivery rule. Do not treat other known requirements or a hidden comment as sufficient. Distinguish this from an explicitly open decision in the meeting, which belongs naturally in the note and does not itself block delivery.

7. Verify that the document starts with valid YAML frontmatter containing `lang` as a BCP 47 tag matching the actual note language under the user/project language policy. Check the presence or absence of `requirements` in YAML `tags` against the note's substantive participant requirements or assessment information using the note standard, including conditional or genuinely open arrangements. Do not infer it from an academic meeting name, incidental keywords, or optional reading, or treat it as proof of completion or a separate assessment. Preserve other tags and metadata. Check for a short factual topic title, optionally followed by two or three key concepts, and a short natural meeting-details line immediately below it. Verify the meeting name and date without inventing missing metadata. The substantive body should start with a concept or problem, not source-processing narration or an appraisal of the lecture. Assess each sentence's function and naturalness, not a list of forbidden words. Remove unnecessary source-review narration about missing coverage or assurances; when a meaningful limit is present, preserve the supported fact directly without adding knowledge or losing conditions. Do not recast processing uncertainty as an intentionally open instructor decision. Reject technical provenance phrases and repetitive reporting of what the instructor said, while preserving necessary attribution of opinions, disputed positions, assessment requirements, and actual announcements about future meetings. Check generated substantive prose for U+2014 and U+2013 and request a plain hyphen or a recast sentence instead.

Report the source ranges actually read, coverage and clarity assessment, and findings labeled `blocker / major / minor`. For each finding give its location, source evidence, and a concrete correction. A blocker is a false central conclusion or fundamentally misleading treatment; major means loss of an important condition, example, or explanation; minor means a local editorial issue without changed meaning. State evaluation limits, including the absence of comparison with the recording.

On recheck, focus on corrections and their effects on neighboring text. Do not reframe new stylistic preferences as source errors.
