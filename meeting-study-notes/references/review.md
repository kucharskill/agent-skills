# Fresh reviewer task

## Skip-review mode

When asked to verify that no note should be created, inspect every source core and ledger sequentially, without requiring a draft. Independently assess whether any explanation, distinction, example, exercise, assignment, assessment instruction, exam information, or deadline is worth retaining. Do not judge by length or the opening alone. Return either a supported skip decision with coverage and reasons, or concrete overlooked material that warrants a note. Distinguish lack of useful content from unreadable or incomplete input. Keep the report in project `.tmp/`; a confirmed skip produces no final note or image directory and must not remove any pre-existing note. Missing or unhelpful screenshots alone never justify skipping useful text.

## Note-review mode

Assess a note written by another author. You receive SKILL.md, the note standard, source manifest, chunks, extraction ledgers, and draft. Do not assume the ledgers are complete. Do not edit the note files. Save a separate report and return concise findings with proposed corrections.

1. Read every core range sequentially, not just a beginning-and-end sample. Compare source, ledger, and note, section by section when needed. Identify important information, examples, and exceptions lost during extraction or synthesis.
2. Check reasoning, conditions, and attribution. Look for student answers turned into established claims, invented conclusions, overgeneralization, and hidden uncertainty. Agreement between two agents is not evidence.
3. Assess standalone clarity and educational usefulness: developed explanations, meaningful exercises, and revision support. Do not reward length or section count alone. Do not demand outside material merely to turn the note into a textbook.
4. Check that visible text contains no timestamps, raw speaker labels, AI narrative, or verification report. Evidence may be in HTML comments; substantive qualifications must remain visible. Check that headings, explanations, and recall questions follow the consuming project's selected language, rather than defaulting to the skill's English.

5. If images are included, inspect every selected image and its relevant original screenshot and transcript excerpt. Check educational or evidentiary value, accurate captions, preserved legends and task data after cropping, readability, placement, portable relative links, and the 10-image maximum. Check project output and filename conventions and shared versus dedicated image folders. Time proximity alone does not establish what a speaker referred to. Do not require images when none are useful or open video to verify them. If optional visual inspection cannot be completed, omit unverified illustrations rather than implying they were reviewed.

6. Check that all supported assignments, assessment requirements, tests, exams, and deadlines appear in a distinct section immediately below the title. Verify exact conditions and dates against the source, including later corrections or cancellations. Confirm that proposals and unresolved questions are not presented as settled instructions, missing details were not invented, and an empty section was not added without relevant source content.

Report the source ranges actually read, coverage and clarity assessment, and findings labeled `blocker / major / minor`. For each finding give its location, source evidence, and a concrete correction. A blocker is a false central conclusion or fundamentally misleading treatment; major means loss of an important condition, example, or explanation; minor means a local editorial issue without changed meaning. State evaluation limits, including the absence of comparison with the recording.

On recheck, focus on corrections and their effects on neighboring text. Do not reframe new stylistic preferences as source errors.
